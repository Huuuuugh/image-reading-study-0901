How we judge your answers

Six rules decide whether an answer counts as right. They are the same on every question in this batch, so read them once here.

  • Scene questions ask whether the picture shows a kind of scene or object at all. Answer yes if it is there in any form, even if details are wrong, ugly or impossible; answer no only if it is missing or the picture shows a completely different scene.
  • When a green rectangle marks one object, judge only what is inside it. One whole object of the kind asked about means yes, even when the rectangle edges touch a neighbour or some background. Answer no when the rectangle holds a second one of them, or only a part of one.
  • Direction questions want one answer even when the thing spreads over a range. Read off the direction at the middle of the marked thing, and give that one.
  • When a question offers cannot tell or none as an answer, and the thing asked about is not visible in the picture you were sent, give that offered answer word for word. It is a correct answer, not a refusal.
  • When a question says to write the number only, type the digits by themselves. Leave out units, currency marks, and any word or letter that labels the number.
  • If you truly cannot read it, use the cannot tell button rather than guessing. A guess on an unreadable picture is worse for this study than saying so.

You will see a series of images (some are close-up cut-outs of a larger picture), each with one short question. Answer exactly what is drawn in the image shown — do not infer what "should" be there, and do not correct anything that looks wrong.

Some cut-outs have a bright green rectangle drawn on them and the rest of the cut-out dimmed. The green rectangle marks the thing the question is about. It was drawn on top afterwards, so never answer with its colour, size or position, and never count it as an object in the picture.

How to answer: when the question offers a list of choices, click one of the choice buttons. Yes-no questions have YES and NO buttons. Everything else is typed in the box and sent with the Submit button. Three buttons sit under every question:

  • Submit — send the answer you typed.
  • I can't tell (the picture doesn't show it) — the thing asked about is not there, is cut off, or cannot be read. This is a correct answer, not a refusal, and it is never counted against you.
    On a clear picture answer yes or no; use can't tell only when the picture truly does not show it. On a clear picture it counts as a wrong answer.
  • Skip this question — leave it unanswered and move on. Only if answering is truly impossible.

Keyboard shortcuts still work if you prefer them: y/n, a/b, 1..9 for the choice buttons, Enter to submit, k to skip. Lists: comma-separated, in the order the question specifies.

Your progress is saved in this browser; you can close and resume. When every question is answered you will get a completion code.
